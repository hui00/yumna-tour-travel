# 3. Unverified content is a draft until the operator approves it

Date: 2026-07-25

## Status

Accepted

## Context

This is the website of a real, operating business with real customers and a 5.0
Google rating from 6 reviews. The source material we were given is about two to
three sentences per service — nowhere near enough to fill ten service pages.

Anything written beyond that source material is, by definition, a guess about
what Muhajir offers. Once published, a guess stops being a guess: a visitor
reads "3 hours, includes lunch and equipment, 450,000 IDR" as a commitment and
messages him on WhatsApp expecting it. He is the one who has to walk it back.
Rinjani trekking makes this sharper still — permits, fitness requirements and
season windows are safety information, not marketing copy.

Options were: publish only what we can source (thin pages, defeating the reason
for per-service pages), pause until Muhajir supplies real details (blocks the
project), or write plausible content under a gate.

## Decision

Draft the pages against Lombok market norms, and gate every unverified claim:

- No prices anywhere. Every service says **on request** and routes to WhatsApp.
- Every unverified specific — duration, inclusions, itinerary, group size,
  season, difficulty — is recorded in a launch approval list.
- Placeholder imagery is recorded in the same list.
- The site does not go live until Muhajir has been through that list.

## Consequences

- The full site is visible and reviewable immediately, without any invented
  detail becoming a promise to a customer.
- The approval list is a real deliverable, not a formality. It is the thing
  Muhajir is actually asked to do, and it grows with every page added.
- "On request" costs some conversions — visitors who only wanted a price will
  leave. Accepted: a wrong price costs more than a missing one.
- Anyone adding a service page later inherits this rule. New specifics go on the
  list; they do not go live unreviewed.
